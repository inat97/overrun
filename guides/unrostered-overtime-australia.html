How to claim unrostered overtime as a junior doctor in Australia

Australia · Updated October 2026

Staying back after handover, coming in early for a ward round, getting called back in: if it was outside your rostered hours, it is unrostered overtime. How you claim it depends on the state you work in and your agreement. This guide covers the parts that apply everywhere, and NSW in detail, where the process is publicly documented.

This guide is general information, written in plain language and checked against the sources linked below as of October 2026. It is not legal, contractual, industrial or financial advice, and rules change. Your contract or agreement, your employer and your union have the final word.

Why it matters now

Unclaimed overtime has been the subject of large class actions. In NSW, a class action on behalf of junior medical officers settled for $229.8 million including legal and other costs; the NSW Supreme Court approved the settlement in August 2024 and the administrator reports that distribution payments were made on 4 August 2026 (Maurice Blackburn). In Victoria, the Federal Court approved a reported $173 million settlement of class actions brought by ASMOF against Victorian health services in late 2025 (HR Leader). Both cases were settled rather than decided at trial. For individual doctors, the practical lesson is the same: a record made at the time is far more useful than a memory years later.

The basics, whatever state you work in

  • Find your claim process in week one. Ask your JMO unit or medical workforce team how overtime is claimed, whether it needs approval, and the deadline.
  • Know which document applies to you. Your pay and overtime rules come from your state award and enterprise or certified agreement, plus your health service's policies — not from a single national rule.
  • Claim every pay period. Claims are easiest to approve when they are recent and match the roster.
  • Keep your own record of rostered and actual times, the reason, and who asked you to stay. See what to record for every overtime shift.
  • Ask your union (AMA or ASMOF in your state) if a claim is refused or you are discouraged from claiming.

New South Wales: how claims work

NSW Health publishes a factsheet for JMOs (last updated October 2023), which points to Policy Directive PD2019_027 for the full rules. As of October 2026, it says:

You are expected to claim

JMOs must claim all hours worked above their rostered hours. Verifying the hours you work is a term of your employment.

Where to claim

In the UROC module of StaffLink or the UROC mobile app. A manual form, sent through your JMO unit, is used only for claims older than 3 months or entered after you signed the declaration.

When to claim

As soon as possible and no later than 4 weeks after the time was worked. UROC accepts claims within 3 months of the end of the pay period; after that you need the manual form and an explanation.

The pay-period declaration

You sign a declaration each pay period confirming you have claimed all additional hours. Once you sign, you can no longer claim that period in UROC. The factsheet is clear: don't sign if you know you have unclaimed hours. Contact your JMO unit, your director of medical services or director of training, or the Ministry's JMO employment team instead. You don't need to tell your supervisor or head of department about declaration issues.

Approval

Some overtime doesn't need prior approval: medical emergencies, patient transfers, late ward rounds, mandatory training and clinical handover. Other unrostered overtime must be approved before you work it, and processes vary between facilities. Record the name and position of the person who approved it in your claim.

Missed meal breaks and meal allowances

You're entitled to a 30-minute meal break. On a Monday–Friday day shift (not a public holiday) the break is unpaid, so if you miss it and can't take it later in the shift, you claim the missed time (1–30 minutes) in UROC. A meal allowance can be claimed only when strict criteria are met, including that the overtime was required and authorised, fell in qualifying hours, and no meal was provided. Callbacks are claimed through the same system.

Victoria, Queensland and other states

Doctors in training in Victorian public health services and Queensland Health medical officers are covered by their own statewide agreements, and the claim process is run by each health service. We could not find an official, public step-by-step guide to the claim process in those states that we could cite, so we won't paraphrase one. Ask your medical workforce unit for the local process and deadline in writing, read the overtime clauses of your agreement, and use your union's member resources. The basics above still apply.

Mistakes that cost doctors money

  • Not claiming because it feels awkward. In NSW, the declaration you sign each pay period says you have claimed everything.
  • Netting it off. Leaving 30 minutes early on Tuesday doesn't cancel staying 2 hours late on Monday unless your agreement says so — record both.
  • No approver recorded. Where approval is needed, write down who gave it, at the time.
  • Patient identifiers in personal notes. If your official claim needs a record number, put it only in the official system, not on your phone.

How Overrun helps

Overrun is an iPhone log for unrostered overtime. After each rostered shift, it asks on the notification whether you left on time, are leaving now, or are still here, and records your actual start and finish, the reason, who authorised it and any missed meal break.

  • It groups shifts by your pay period (weekly, fortnightly or monthly) and keeps a running total of extra time you haven't claimed yet — useful before you sign a declaration.
  • You can set a claim window, such as NSW's four weeks (28 days), and it warns you a week before a shift reaches it.
  • It builds a PDF and CSV of every shift, to copy from when you enter claims in your health service's system or to hand to your JMO unit.

What it does not do: it doesn't connect to StaffLink or any payroll system, lodge claims, or work out what you're owed under your award. If you enter an hourly rate it shows your own estimate, labelled as one.
