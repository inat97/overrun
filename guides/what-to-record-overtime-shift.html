What to record for every overtime shift

UK and Australia · Updated October 2026

Whether you're filing an exception report in England or an unrostered overtime claim in Australia, the claim is checked against the same thing: what you were rostered to work, and what you can show you actually worked. Here's a checklist of what to write down, and why each item matters.

This guide is general information, written in plain language and checked against the sources linked below as of October 2026. It is not legal, contractual, industrial or financial advice, and rules change. Your contract or agreement, your employer and your union have the final word.

Why a record made at the time matters

In England, HR checks every exception report by cross-checking three things: your report, your rota, and evidence of the hours. If they don't line up, you are contacted to correct or justify it (framework agreement). In NSW, you sign a declaration each pay period confirming you've claimed all your hours (NSW Health). In both systems, notes written on the day make the claim quick to check; vague recollections weeks later make it slow, or impossible.

The checklist

1. Date and rostered hours

The shift date and your rostered start and finish, as they were on the day. Rosters change, and the claim is measured against the one you were actually working to. In England you'll need a copy of your rota for each report (BMA).

2. Actual start and finish

The time you actually started and actually left, written down then, not reconstructed. Note early starts as well as late finishes — both count. Record a callback as its own entry with its own times.

3. Evidence of time and place, if your system asks for it

England's reformed process requires evidence of the time, date and location of the additional hours; the BMA gives a screenshot of a maps app as an example, and your trust may provide its own method. For non-resident on-call work, the framework says phone call logs may support the check. Find out what your employer accepts before you need it.

4. The reason, in general terms

Handover ran over, an unwell patient, admissions, a late ward round, covering a gap, paperwork. In NSW, some reasons, such as clinical handover, late ward rounds and medical emergencies, don't need prior approval; others do. In England, reports fall into categories such as an unscheduled late finish or being unable to take breaks.

5. Who asked you to stay, or approved it

A name and role, or just the role (for example, "on-call registrar"). NSW Health asks you to record the name and position of the person who approved the overtime when approval is required.

6. Missed breaks

Whether you missed your meal break and couldn't take it later. Being unable to take contractual breaks is a reportable category in England; in NSW, a missed meal break on a weekday day shift can be claimed in its own right.

7. Pay or time off

In England, you choose payment or time off in lieu for each report, unless a safe working hours breach makes time off mandatory. Note which you chose.

8. What happened to the claim

Date submitted, and the outcome: paid, given as time off, declined, or still pending. Without this, it's easy to lose track of claims that were never paid, or to claim the same shift twice.

What not to record

Anything that identifies a patient — names, record numbers, dates of birth, bed numbers combined with details. A general reason is enough for a personal log. If an official claim system asks for a record number, as NSW's can where relevant, put it only there.

Also leave out commentary about colleagues. Keep the record factual: times, reasons, who asked.

How long to keep it

At least until each claim is paid or settled, and ideally much longer: underpayment disputes can take years to resolve, as the recent Australian class actions show. Exporting a copy each pay period costs nothing.

How Overrun helps

Overrun is built around this checklist. For each shift it keeps the rostered and actual times, the reason (from general categories, with no space for patient details), who asked you to stay, whether you missed a meal break, and the claim status: not claimed, submitted, paid, time off in lieu, declined or not claiming.

  • A notification after each rostered finish lets you log "left on time" or "leaving now" in one tap, so the times are recorded on the day.
  • It counts extra time as minutes before the rostered start plus minutes after the rostered end, never netting off early finishes, and prints that method on every claim pack.
  • Claim packs are a PDF and a CSV for any pay period, which you can save or send yourself.

Everything stays on your iPhone: no account, no server. It doesn't record your location, so if your employer asks for location evidence, use the method they provide.
