How to submit an exception report in England (2026 rules)

England only · Updated October 2026

If you are a resident doctor in England and you stayed late, started early, missed a break or lost teaching, an exception report is how you tell your employer — and how you get paid or given time back. The process changed on 4 February 2026. Here is how it works now.

This guide is general information, written in plain language and checked against the sources linked below as of October 2026. It is not legal, contractual, industrial or financial advice, and rules change. Your contract or agreement, your employer and your union have the final word.

What changed in February 2026

Exception reporting is part of the 2016 terms and conditions of service (TCS) for resident doctors (formerly junior doctors) in England. As part of the 2024 pay deal, the BMA's resident doctors committee accepted a framework agreement reforming it on 31 March 2025, and every employer had to have the new process in place by 4 February 2026 (NHS Employers).

According to the BMA's summary, the biggest practical changes are:

  • Your clinical and educational supervisors no longer sign off reports of additional hours. Hours reports go to HR (medical workforce) with the guardian of safe working hours overseeing them; educational reports go to the director of medical education.
  • You have 28 days from the day it happened to submit.
  • There is no pre-authorisation, and no in-person meeting is needed to get a report approved.
  • Employers face fines if you cannot get access to the reporting system, or if your reports are shared in a way that harms you.

Who can exception report

All resident doctors employed on the 2016 TCS in England. The BMA says employers are strongly encouraged to extend access to other groups, such as locally employed doctors and academic trainees, so if you are not on the 2016 TCS, ask your medical workforce team whether you have access. If you work in Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland, this guide does not apply: check your own contract.

What you can report

The framework agreement (paragraph 40) says reporting systems must include, at a minimum, categories for:

  • an unscheduled early start or late finish
  • being unable to take your contractual breaks
  • inadequate clinical support or rostered skills mix
  • missed educational opportunities
  • breaches of a non-resident on-call pattern
  • a suspected non-compliant rota pattern
  • detriment or threat of detriment, and information breaches

Step by step

  1. Get access in your first week. Your employer should give you access within 7 calendar days of starting a new post, site or employer. If it doesn't, raise it with HR and the guardian of safe working hours; if it is still unresolved 7 days later, the BMA says a weekly fine applies. Systems vary between trusts, so learn yours before you need it.
  2. Capture evidence at the time. The framework says HR checks three things: your report, your rota, and evidence of the additional hours — time, date and location. The BMA gives a screenshot of a maps app as an example. Your trust may provide its own way of doing this. If you cannot or would rather not use it, you may ask another regulated professional to corroborate by email, but that cannot be made a requirement.
  3. Submit as soon as you can, and no later than 28 days from the day it happened. The deadline can be relaxed only for reasons outside your control, as decided by the guardian.
  4. Fill in only what is needed. Mandatory fields are meant to be limited to who you are, the date the shift started, the rota, the category, whether there was an immediate safety concern, the minimum needed to calculate the hours, and whether you want pay or time off. You confirm by self-declaration that the report is accurate. Several occurrences in one shift should ideally go on one form.
  5. Choose pay or time off in lieu (TOIL). Your choice must be respected unless a breach of safe working hours means TOIL is mandatory. Mandatory TOIL should be arranged within one day of the award, requested TOIL within 10 days.

What happens after you submit

The framework sets out three levels:

  • Level 0: HR cross-checks your report, evidence and rota. If they match, it goes to payroll or TOIL is approved. This is expected to cover the vast majority of reports.
  • Level 1: only if something doesn't match, HR contacts you. You can correct and resubmit, withdraw, or confirm that the report is accurate.
  • Level 2: if HR still rejects it and you stand by it, the guardian of safe working hours reviews the evidence and makes the decision. No in-person meeting is required.

For two hours or less of additional work on one occasion, the only question is whether you actually worked it — not whether, looking back, you should have stayed. Longer occurrences are investigated to make sure staffing is safe, under a local process agreed with the local negotiating committee. Reports should be actioned within 10 days; the BMA says this falls to 7 days from August 2026.

Safe working limits worth knowing

The BMA's rota checker summarises the main 2016 TCS limits: an average of no more than 48 hours a week, no more than 72 hours in any 168 hours, shifts of no more than 13 hours, and at least 11 hours' continuous rest between rostered shifts. They matter for more than pay: the BMA says breaching the 48-hour average or the 72-hour limit triggers a guardian fine, and a breach of the 11-hour rest means time off in lieu, to be taken within 24 hours.

Mistakes that cost doctors hours

  • Waiting until the end of the rotation. With a 28-day limit, a run of busy weeks can push early shifts out of time.
  • Relying on memory. "About an hour late most nights" is hard to report. Exact times written down that day are not.
  • Forgetting early starts and missed breaks. They are reportable categories too.
  • Putting patient details in free text. A general reason, such as an unwell patient or a handover that ran over, is enough.
